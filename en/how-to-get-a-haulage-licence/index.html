How to get a road haulage licence in the Czech Republic

The whole process, from the conditions and the application at the trade licensing office to the EU Community licence. For road haulage over 3.5 t and for 2.5–3.5 t vans that carry goods abroad.

1. Four conditions you must meet

The conditions are set by EU Regulation 1071/2009 and the Czech Road Transport Act. The authority checks them in the same way for companies and for sole traders.

Establishment

A registered office or premises in the Czech Republic that you are allowed to use, and at least one vehicle at your disposal: owned, leased, or rented (then the contract is attached).

Good repute

Neither the company nor the transport manager may have serious infringements, for example in road transport, employment law or business law.

Financial standing

EUR 9,000 for the first vehicle over 3.5 t and EUR 5,000 for each additional one. Proven by annual accounts, or more simply by financial standing insurance or a bank guarantee.

Professional competence

A certificate of professional competence. If you do not have one, you appoint a transport manager. We provide one.

2. Step by step

  1. You agree with a transport manager

    Send us a free enquiry. Within 3 working days of receiving your documents you get the transport manager contract and the declaration to attach to your application.

  2. You apply for the licence

    At any Czech trade licensing office, at a Czech POINT, or through a data box. The trade is road motor transport (silniční motorová doprava) for vehicles over 3.5 t. The administrative fee is CZK 1,000 (CZK 500 for each further application). You attach documents for all four conditions.

  3. The transport authority gives its opinion

    The trade licensing office asks the transport authority (regional authority, or the City of Prague) for its opinion; it checks the conditions. If something is missing, they contact you and you add the documents.

  4. The office decides

    The trade licensing office decides within 30 days of receiving a complete application. The licence is entered in the Trade Register together with the transport manager.

  5. You register your vehicles

    You report the vehicles you will use to the transport authority. Financial standing must be proven for each of them.

  6. Community licence, if you go abroad

    With the licence, you apply to the transport authority for the Community licence (in Czech eurolicence). You receive the original and a certified copy for each vehicle.

4. Vans 2.5–3.5 t going abroad

Since 21 May 2022, vans over 2.5 t that carry goods for others abroad must meet the same four conditions as trucks. Financial standing is lower: EUR 1,800 for the first vehicle and EUR 900 for each additional one. The Community licence for vans is marked “≤ 3.5t”.

Since 1 July 2026 these vans must have a second-generation smart tachograph and follow the driving and rest time rules.

You need a transport manager for a van only if you go abroad. Vans that only operate within the Czech Republic do not need professional competence.

5. Obligations after you get the licence

  • Financial standing every year must be proven to the transport authority by 31 July. For our hauliers we keep track of it and arrange the insurance.
  • Tachograph data must be downloaded regularly: from the driver card at least every 28 days, from the vehicle at least every 90 days.
  • Report changes to the authorities, for example new vehicles or a change of transport manager.
  • The transport manager really manages the transport: supervises vehicles, drivers and documents. It is not just “lending” a certificate.

6. When your current transport manager leaves

Without a transport manager you may not continue operating. The law gives you 15 days to appoint a new one. Contact us as soon as possible: we prepare the contract and declaration right away and help with the change at the trade licensing office.

7. For foreigners

The Czech licence is issued only to a business established in the Czech Republic. As a foreigner you have these options:

  • A Czech limited company (s.r.o.) that you set up or buy.
  • A branch (odštěpný závod) of your foreign company registered in the Czech Commercial Register.
  • A Czech sole trader registration, if your residence status allows it.

In each case the business needs premises in the Czech Republic and vehicles registered here. Citizens of countries outside the EU should also check their residence and business permit conditions before starting.

A Czech licence cannot be lent or rented to a company registered in another country. We only work as transport manager for businesses that genuinely operate from the Czech Republic.
